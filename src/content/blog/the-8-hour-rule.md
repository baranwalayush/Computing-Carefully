Hi all,

This will probably be my first blog here, and I must say, I'm not particularly excited about it. We'll get into the reasons shortly. Also, just to set expectations, this isn't going to be a technical blog.
<br><br>
**TL;DR:** I'm tired. And the fact that most of us are either studying, working, or preparing ourselves to get into this exact life is what concerns me even more.
<br><br>
So, very recently, I joined my first on-site, full-time internship at a startup working in the domain of Electronic Design Automation (EDA). Happy news, right? Absolutely! I'm genuinely happy about landing a good internship, especially after struggling to find one during the summer. But over the past couple of weeks, there have been a few things I haven't been able to stop thinking about.

1. What does being employed actually mean, and why is it so important (or is it)?
2. Where exactly is humanity heading?

I'll try to put some of my thoughts into words here. So, let's dive right in.

### The 8-hour rule

Being employed, in its simplest sense, means that you're working for someone or something in exchange for monetary benefits. In most conventional jobs, that work lasts for around 8 hours (9 to 5 or 10 to 6), sometimes extending to 9 hours or even more. (I'm mostly talking about the kind of jobs I'm familiar with. I understand that there are professions where people put in considerably more hours.)
<br><br>
There's this idea I've come across somewhere that a human's day is divided into three equal parts: 8 hours of work, 8 hours of rest, and 8 hours of personal life. Sounds lovely, doesn't it? Almost like a perfectly balanced life. But it starts looking a little different when you try to look into the gaps.
<br><br>
Think about it. Even when I wasn't working, I still slept for around 8 hours. And I didn't have nearly as many responsibilities or things to worry about back then. Which means that irrespective of whether you're employed or not, you still need those 8 hours of sleep to maintain a healthy life.
<br><br>
So, when you're employed, those 8 hours of rest aren't really something you get in addition to your existing routine. They're already accounted for. The real time you have to yourself is what's left after work and sleep.
<br><br>
And that's where things get interesting.

### The hidden cost of an 8-hour shift

When you're employed, there are several hidden costs on top of those 8 working hours. You have to get ready, commute, plan your day around work, and adjust your personal routine accordingly. You can't simply decide to do something because you feel like it. You can't go to bed late just because you want to. You have to follow a routine irrespective of how you feel, what the weather is like, or what's happening around you.
<br><br>
All of these things take time, and they add up.
<br><br>
For example, in my case, I spend almost 2 hours travelling to work and another 2 hours getting back home. That's 4 additional hours gone every single day, just to make those 8 working hours possible.
<br><br>
Then there are the daily chores. Getting ready in the morning, having meals, and all the other little things that are part of everyday life. These easily take another 2 hours or so.
<br><br>
Let's do some basic arithmetic:
- 8 hours of work
- 8 hours of sleep
- 4 hours of commute
- 2 hours of daily chores and routines

That leaves me with roughly **2 hours of genuinely personal time** in an entire day.
<br><br>
And that's the exact thing that's been bothering me. In your case, if your commute is shorter, you might have around 4 hours. But even then, think about how little time that actually is.

### Is this really what we're working towards?

How did we get to a point where almost everybody is either working, studying, or training themselves to eventually enter this exact same loop?
<br><br>
The very concept feels somewhat absurd to me.
<br><br>
Think about it. All my friends, including me, and practically everyone around us who has been going to school, preparing for competitive exams, attending universities, and studying for the past 10–15 years, are ultimately working towards getting a job. And what does that job look like for most of us? An 8-hour shift, five days a week, for the foreseeable future.
<br><br>
When did we collectively decide that we'd dedicate 5/7 of our week to work and leave just 2/7 (Saturday and Sunday) to actually live?
<br><br>
And I do understand the irony here. I'm able to question all of this partly because I've managed to get an internship. Had I not, I'd probably still be looking for one, worrying about my career and hoping to get into this very same system. I'm as much a part of this society as anyone else.
<br><br>
But my thoughts aren't really about my internship, the work I'm doing, or even whether I enjoy it. In fact, I'm quite happy with the opportunity. It's simply about questioning the larger construct we've built around our lives.
<br><br>
I also understand that I don't have an alternative to propose. Society has to function, businesses have to operate, and people have to work for things to keep moving. I get that. But somehow, I'm still not entirely convinced that this is the best way we've figured out to live.
<br><br>
Maybe I'm just too new to all of this, and these thoughts will change as I get more accustomed to working life. Maybe there's something I'm not seeing yet. But for now, I can't help but wonder if we're spending so much of our lives working towards a future in which we barely have the time to enjoy living.
<br><br>
Anyway, just to end on a positive note, keep hustling and keep looking for better opportunities that make your life easier to **live**.
